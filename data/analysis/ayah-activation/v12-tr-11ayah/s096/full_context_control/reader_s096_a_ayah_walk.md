# Surah 96 Full-Context Control: Eleven-Ayah Ayah Walk

Each fixed ayah is read within its clipped eleven-ayah focus window: the ayah itself, up to five predecessors, and up to five successors.

## 96:0 — بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

### Activated readings

1. **An operative designation, not a detached label.** Lexically, `س م و:B005` supplies “name as raised designation,” sharpened by `س م و:B001` (“rising high”). Structurally, 96:1 repeats the exact `باسم` construction and attaches it to `اقرأ`, after which creating and teaching unfold (96:1–5). The designation therefore serves as the governing banner under which speech and formation proceed. The packet supplies the repetition and sequence; I infer from repeated `بـ` that the designation authorizes or enables the acts. The basmalah consequently reads as entry into an operative agency, not merely a prefatory naming.

2. **Mercy as a generative enclosure.** `ر ح م:B001` supplies tenderness and beneficence, while `ر ح م:B003` supplies the womb as a vessel of growth. The doubled adjectives precede creation from `علق` (96:2) and staged teaching (96:4–5). The womb branch gives mercy a material role: it contains, sustains, and develops what begins in dependence. The packet does not say that mercy causes those stages; I infer that creation and instruction instantiate the kind of protected development pictured by the womb. Thus `الرحمن الرحيم` acquires a formative, womb-like force in addition to affective compassion.

3. **Invocation exported into the following action.** `ء ل ه:B002` identifies `الله` as the fixed divine name used in invocation, while `ء ل ه:B001` supplies the worshipped one. The immediate recurrence of `باسم` in 96:1 turns the opening invocation into the grammatical source-frame for recitation. I infer that the shared construction carries the invocation forward rather than restarting an unrelated formula. The line reads as an act of orientation toward the worshipped source whose name governs the ensuing command.

### Retrospective surprises

96:1’s exact repetition of `باسم` confirmed that the opening designation is carried into action rather than left as a heading. More unexpectedly, 96:2 and 96:4–5 activated the womb variant `ر ح م:B003`: attachment, formation, and teaching made the doubled mercy developmental and containing, not only affective. No later ayah within 96:0’s clipped window corrected those activations.

## 96:1 — ٱقْرَأْ بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ

### Activated readings

1. **Recitation as the gathering of a whole.** The gathering-speech variant of `ق ر ء:B001` joins collecting with articulated recitation. In 96:2–5, material origin, repeated `اقرأ`, pen, and previously unknown knowledge are placed around the command. The branch’s role is to make reading an assembly operation: dispersed signs, origin, and taught content are gathered into ordered utterance. The packet gives the sequence; I infer that the repeated imperative asks for integrative articulation rather than bare vocalization. `اقرأ` therefore reads as “gather into a recitable whole” as well as “recite.”

2. **Creation as measured, continuing nurture.** `خ ل ق:B001` supplies measuring and proportioning before execution, `خ ل ق:B002` originating, and `ر ب ب:B002` repair, nurture, and stage-by-stage completion. The relative clause binds `ربك` directly to `خلق`, while 96:2 specifies a dependent material beginning and 96:4–5 continue the process through instruction. I infer that the creator’s initial proportioning continues as the Rabb’s developmental care. The clause thus reads less like a past credential and more like the opening phase of sustained formation.

3. **A named authority that blocks autonomous elevation.** `س م و:B005` makes `اسم` a raised designation, and `ر ب ب:B001` supplies ownership and obeyed authority. This invocation precedes the repeated reading command, whereas 96:6 presents the human crossing bounds. I infer that acting `باسم ربك` assigns the act’s authority and elevation to the Rabb rather than to the reader. The phrase becomes an anti-autonomous condition on knowledge: recitation is elevated by whose designation it bears, not by the speaker’s self-importance.

4. **A created measure against which transgression becomes legible.** `خ ل ق:B001` provides proportion, while `خ ل ق:B004` provides formed disposition and moral bearing. The window moves from creation and teaching to the human’s `يطغى` in 96:6, whose `ط غ ي:B001` is overstepping a bound. The supplied facts are measure and later boundary-crossing; I infer that the created proportion supplies the normative line being crossed. `خلق` consequently includes the establishment of an apt order that human conduct can violate, without implying that creation forces the violation.

### Retrospective surprises

96:4–5 made the gathering variant of `ق ر ء:B001` newly concrete by supplying pen-made signs and acquired knowledge to assemble. The sharper surprise came in 96:6: `يطغى` retrospectively turned the measuring branch `خ ل ق:B001` into a moral boundary and `باسم ربك` into protection against self-authorized elevation. This corrected an initially narrower reading of `خلق` as origin alone.

## 96:2 — خَلَقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ

### Activated readings

1. **Constitutive dependence rather than a discarded first stage.** `ع ل ق:B001` supplies something clinging to or hanging from another, and `ع ل ق:B009` supplies conception taking hold. The same human is taught in 96:5, crosses bounds in 96:6, and sees himself as self-sufficient in 96:7 (`غ ن ي:B001`). The attachment branch functions as the human’s dependency architecture; the later self-assessment is its exact reversal. I infer that origin remains diagnostic of present status rather than becoming irrelevant after growth. The ayah therefore reads as a standing contradiction of imagined independence.

2. **Formation as a passage from attachment into proportioned presence.** `خ ل ق:B001` supplies measuring, `خ ل ق:B002` bringing into being, and `خ ل ق:B003` complete, well-proportioned form; `ع ل ق:B003` supplies congealed blood and a blood-sucking attached body. The sequence continues from this compact attached material to instruction by pen and acquired knowledge (96:4–5). I infer that the juxtaposed stages describe one formative trajectory. `خلق` then highlights transformation and proportioning, while `علق` keeps the materially dependent starting condition visible inside the finished human.

3. **The human as a perceiving image who can misread himself (exploratory).** `ء ن س:B002` supplies perception entering awareness, and `ء ن س:B005` the small human image visible in the pupil. In 96:5–7, `الإنسان` repeats before the reflexive `رآه`: the human learns, then sees himself as sufficient. The eye-image branch acts as a mirror model in which a visible image is mistaken for the whole perceiving self. I infer, more tentatively, that the lexical eye-image sharpens the reflexive grammar. The fixed `الإنسان` can thus carry an ironic vulnerability: the perceiver may absolutize his own image.

### Retrospective surprises

96:7 supplied the decisive retrospective reversal: the being made from clinging `علق` judges himself independent. Its reflexive `رآه` also made the pupil-image branch `ء ن س:B005` visible, while 96:4–5 confirmed that attachment opens into a teachable developmental sequence. The later context therefore preserved dependence as a present diagnosis rather than a superseded biological stage.

## 96:3 — ٱقْرَأْ وَرَبُّكَ ٱلْأَكْرَمُ

### Activated readings

1. **A second gathering after origin has been disclosed.** The collection/recitation variant of `ق ر ء:B001` is reactivated by the exact repetition of `اقرأ` from 96:1, now after 96:2 has supplied the human’s attached origin. `ق ر ء:B002` also contains an appointed cycle variant. The second imperative’s role is iterative: the material origin must now be gathered into the act of reading. I infer from repetition across intervening content that this is a renewed pass, not redundant emphasis. The command reads as rereading after a change in self-understanding.

2. **Generosity as cultivation.** `ك ر م:B001` supplies praiseworthy generosity, while `ك ر م:B002` supplies rain-bearing bounty and fertile growth; `ر ب ب:B002` supplies stage-by-stage nurture. Creation from attachment precedes the line and teaching by pen follows it. The growth branch makes `الأكرم` the productive condition under which a dependent being develops into a knower. I infer that teaching plays the role of rain-like provision in this local mechanism. The superlative therefore evokes not only lavish giving but cultivation that makes latent capacity grow.

3. **A gift-and-return circuit without reducing generosity to payment.** `ك ر م:B008` supplies a gift offered with return or recompense in view, while `ك ر م:B001` preserves non-reductive noble generosity. The Rabb gives reading and knowledge (96:3–5), the human imagines independence (96:6–7), and `الرجعى` returns to the Rabb (96:8). The gift branch’s role is to make reception answerable. The packet supplies giving and return; I infer that the return entails response to the gift, not that the gift was a commercial exchange. `الأكرم` can thus activate both sheer bounty and bounty that creates responsibility.

4. **The Rabb as both cultivator and terminal authority.** `ر ب ب:B002` supplies nurture and completion, while `ر ب ب:B001` supplies lordship and ownership. The same `ربك` that accompanies reading here reappears as the destination of `الرجعى` in 96:8. I infer that return completes the developmental relation begun by nurture. The line reads with a double horizon: the Rabb generously develops the reader and remains the authority to whom that developed agency returns.

### Retrospective surprises

96:6–7 corrected a simple “abundant giving” reading: generosity can be received and then misrecognized as self-possession. 96:8 unexpectedly activated the recompense-bearing gift branch `ك ر م:B008` and joined it to the Rabb’s ownership, so bounty became answerable without ceasing to be `ك ر م:B001` noble generosity.

## 96:4 — ٱلَّذِى عَلَّمَ بِٱلْقَلَمِ

### Activated readings

1. **The pen as a tool that forms by selective cutting.** `ق ل م:B003` supplies the trimmed writing reed, while `ق ل م:B001` supplies trimming or paring a hard thing bit by bit. The pen follows measured creation (`خ ل ق:B001`) and precedes teaching what was unknown (96:5). The cutting branch gives the instrument an active formative role: it segments, shapes, and regularizes material into marks. The packet supplies the material analogy; I infer that pen-mediated teaching works through selection and articulation. The ayah therefore presents instruction as disciplined formation, not mere storage.

2. **Knowing externalized as a distinguishing mark.** `ع ل م:B002` supplies a visible mark that distinguishes and points, and `ق ل م:B003` supplies the writing instrument. Later in the window, `رآه` and `أرأيت` (96:7, 96:9) foreground the limits of immediate seeing. The mark branch’s role is to let knowledge persist and direct attention beyond the present visual field. I infer that written marks extend perception and make distinctions inspectable. `علّم بالقلم` thus reads as teaching through durable signs, not only through an object called a pen.

3. **Disclosure that enables judgment but does not compel it.** `ع ل م:B001` covers making a thing clear, learning, and teaching. The sequence moves from divine teaching (96:4–5) to a human misjudging himself as sufficient and then restraining another (96:6–9). The branch supplies disclosure; the later conduct shows that disclosure can be received without governing self-assessment. I infer a distinction between epistemic capacity and ethical use. The ayah reads as the provision of intelligibility whose misuse cannot be blamed on an absence of teaching.

### Retrospective surprises

The later sight sequence in 96:7 and 96:9 made `ع ل م:B002` more than a graphic analogy: pen-made marks become a corrective to self-enclosed seeing. Meanwhile, 96:6–9 showed that taught intelligibility does not compel sound self-judgment. That later failure sharpened, rather than canceled, the reading of the pen as a disciplined instrument of distinction.

## 96:5 — عَلَّمَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ

### Activated readings

1. **Acquired knowledge as evidence against self-sufficiency.** `ع ل م:B001` covers teaching, learning, and a thing becoming clear. The same root frames the human with causative `علّم` and negated simple `لم يعلم`; 96:7 then says that he sees himself as independent. The form contrast assigns knowledge’s arrival to teaching and its prior absence to the learner. The packet supplies that asymmetry; I infer that received knowledge is evidence of continuing dependence. The line therefore undercuts the later self-sufficient self-image from inside the human’s own knowing.

2. **Teaching as making discriminable signs.** `ع ل م:B002` supplies the distinguishing mark that points to something. The preceding `بالقلم` gives a mark-making instrument, while 96:7 and 96:9 turn to seeing and evaluative attention. The branch’s role is to make teaching a process of marking differences so that an unknown can be recognized. I infer that the pen’s durable distinctions mediate the passage from `لم يعلم` to knowledge. The ayah reads as the production of discernibility, not simply the transfer of propositions.

3. **A perceiver whose awareness can still curve back into error.** `ء ن س:B002` supplies perceiving or bringing something into awareness. The fixed `الإنسان` stands between taught knowledge and the reflexive `رآه` of 96:7; the same human then becomes the subject of boundary-crossing in 96:6. The perception branch makes the human an awareness-bearing recipient, while the sequence distinguishes awareness from accurate self-judgment. I infer that the taught capacity can be redirected onto a false estimate of the self. The line thus makes later transgression an abuse of awakened perception rather than simple unawareness.

### Retrospective surprises

96:7 was the strongest later activation: `استغنى` made the causative/simple contrast in `علّم ... لم يعلم` an internal refutation of independence. 96:9–10 then made the distinguishing-mark branch `ع ل م:B002` ethically consequential, because a taught perceiver must distinguish restraining worship from sound restraint. The later scene corrected any equation of teaching with mere information uptake.

## 96:6 — كَلَّآ إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَيَطْغَىٰٓ

### Activated readings

1. **Received abundance becoming overflow.** `ط غ ي:B002` supplies water or force rising beyond measure and overwhelming its bounds; `ط غ ي:B001` supplies the corresponding moral overstep. The line follows creation, generosity, pen, and teaching, then 96:7 identifies a self-sufficient self-perception. The overflow image makes received capacity the “water” that exceeds its channel when treated as self-owned. The packet does not state that teaching causes rebellion; I infer that abundance without acknowledged dependence enables the excess. `ليطغى` consequently reads as gifted power spilling beyond its proper containment.

2. **Transgression materializing as rival authority.** `ط غ ي:B003` supplies a false authority or deviant leader who turns others from good. In 96:9–10, the overstepping human becomes one who restrains a servant at prayer, and 96:11 places the servant on guidance. Those later roles give the branch a social function: the transgressor does not merely exceed a private limit but installs himself as a gatekeeper over another’s devotion. I infer the identity across the continuous sequence. The general statement therefore acquires an institutional reading: excess hardens into unauthorized command.

3. **The visible human forgetting the attached human.** `ء ن س:B001` supplies visible human presence, while the window retains the human’s origin from clinging `علق` (96:2) and immediately gives reflexive sight (96:7). The fixed `الإنسان` is thus both socially manifest and materially dependent. I infer that prominence in the visible scene helps conceal the dependence from which it arose. The line reads as an irony of human presence: becoming conspicuous can support the illusion that one is unconditioned.

### Retrospective surprises

96:7 supplied the immediate hydraulic trigger: abundance becomes overflow when the receiver judges it self-sufficient. More surprisingly, 96:9–11 gave `ط غ ي:B003` a concrete social body in the person who blocks a praying servant positioned on guidance. The later ayat corrected a merely private vice-reading into one of rival authority exercised over another.

## 96:7 — أَن رَّءَاهُ ٱسْتَغْنَىٰٓ

### Activated readings

1. **A causal loop driven by judgment, not verified independence.** `ر ء ي:B002` supplies opinion and reflective judgment, while `غ ن ي:B001` supplies wealth or lack of need. The reflexive object in `رآه` makes the human both assessor and assessed; repeated `أرأيت` in 96:9 and 96:11 then introduces an external counter-assessment. The packet supplies self-seeing as the stated trigger; I infer that the decisive event is a judgment of sufficiency, not sufficiency independently established. The line therefore diagnoses transgression as an epistemic feedback loop.

2. **Self-sufficiency as attempted substitution.** `غ ن ي:B002` includes sufficing, availing, or standing in for another. The human originates in attachment (96:2), receives teaching (96:4–5), and is immediately told that return is to the Rabb (96:8). The replacement branch’s role is to expose what the reflexive judgment attempts: the self is treated as if it could stand in for every sustaining source. The packet gives dependence and return; I infer the attempted substitution. `استغنى` thus reads as a claim of functional replacement, not only possession of abundance.

3. **A mirror-image challenged by a counter-gaze.** `ر ء ي:B006` supplies visible appearance and mirror, and `ر ء ي:B013` supplies the alerting “tell me” formula. Reflexive `رآه` is followed by two `أرأيت` prompts inside the window (96:9, 96:11). The mirror branch makes the first gaze self-enclosed; the later formulae break that enclosure by making another observer judge the conduct. I infer that the repeated root deliberately changes the direction of scrutiny. The ayah reads as self-image mistaken for reality until a counter-gaze is summoned.

### Retrospective surprises

96:8 immediately exposed `غ ن ي:B002` as attempted replacement by restoring direction to the Rabb. Then 96:9 and 96:11 repeated `أرأيت`, breaking the closed mirror of `رآه`; 96:12 supplied protected self-guarding as an alternative to imagined self-sufficiency. The later counter-gaze was the main surprise and confirmed that the error lies in self-assessment.

## 96:8 — إِنَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ

### Activated readings

1. **Return to source, owner, and completion.** `ر ج ع:B001` supplies return to a prior place, owner, or state; `ر ب ب:B001` supplies ownership and authority, while `ر ب ب:B002` supplies nurture and completion. The directional `إلى` immediately reverses 96:7’s self-sufficient closure, and the earlier generous-teaching relation remains in the window (96:3–5). The Rabb functions both as cultivating giver and destination. I infer that return completes the dependency relation rather than merely changing location. The line reads as restoration to the authority and developmental source the self had mentally displaced.

2. **Return as an answer sent back.** `ر ج ع:B005` supplies a returned answer or reply. The surrounding discourse repeatedly commands (`اقرأ`), then asks `أرأيت` and tests alternatives of guidance, protective command, denial, and turning (96:9–13). The speech structure gives return the role of response: conduct becomes the answer sent back to the Rabb. The packet supplies questions and directives; I infer accountability as the directional arrow joining them to `الرجعى`. The noun can therefore denote responsive reckoning as well as homeward movement.

3. **A corrective turning set against turning away.** `ر ج ع:B003` supplies turning back from an act or prior course. Within the window, 96:13 ends with `تولى`, whose `و ل ي:B007` is turning away. These are opposed vectors: one can reverse a wrong course, whereas the denier reverses away from guidance. I infer that the nominal `الرجعى` keeps corrective reversal latent even while asserting unavoidable return. The line reads simultaneously as destination and as the possibility or demand of course-correction.

### Retrospective surprises

The repeated evaluative prompts in 96:9, 96:11, and 96:13 unexpectedly activated `ر ج ع:B005`: return acquired the form of an answer sent back through conduct. 96:13’s `تولى` also made `ر ج ع:B003` newly visible as the opposite corrective vector. The later ayat broadened `الرجعى` from destination alone to response and course-reversal.

## 96:9 — أَرَءَيْتَ ٱلَّذِى يَنْهَىٰ

### Activated readings

1. **A summons to render judgment.** `ر ء ي:B013` supplies `أرأيت` as an alerting “tell me” formula, while `ر ء ي:B002` supplies reflective judgment. The formula recurs in 96:11 and 96:13 before 96:14 closes with `الله يرى`. Its role is therefore not limited to asking whether an event entered the eye; it recruits the addressee as an evaluator and progressively prepares a judgment under a superior gaze. I infer this discourse function from the repetition. The ayah reads like the opening of an examination or witness summons.

2. **Reason’s restraining function turned against the right act.** `ن ه ي:B001` supplies forbidding and restraining, whereas `ن ه ي:B003` supplies reason named for restraining its owner from ugly action. The object disclosed in 96:10 is a servant praying; 96:11–12 place guidance and protective caution on that side. The intellect branch gives `ينهى` an inverted role: a faculty meant to stop wrong is deployed to stop worship. The packet supplies the moral alignment; I infer functional corruption rather than mere absence of reason. The prohibition becomes a reversal of restraint’s proper purpose.

3. **An attempted endpoint imposed on another’s trajectory.** `ن ه ي:B002` supplies the end, edge, or target reached by a thing. The verb opens onto `عبداً إذا صلى` (96:10), while 96:11 describes being on a directed guidance (`ه د ي:B002`). The prohibitor tries to make prayer terminate and to set the endpoint of another’s course. The packet gives stopping and directed movement; I infer the terminating function. The ayah thus reads spatially as an unauthorized attempt to cut off a guided path.

4. **The watcher becoming watched.** `ر ء ي:B004` supplies mutual visibility and facing. The addressee is told to look at the restrainer; the scene then culminates in `بأن الله يرى` (96:14). The visibility branch makes observation relational rather than one-way: the apparent overseer of the praying servant is himself exposed. I infer the reversal of surveillance from the paired seeing constructions. `أرأيت` therefore begins the transfer of visual authority away from the human controller.

### Retrospective surprises

96:10–12 identified what is being stopped as prayer, guidance, and protective command; this made `ن ه ي:B003` a sharp inversion of reason’s proper restraining work. 96:14 then completed the visual reversal begun by `أرأيت`: the human overseer is seen by Allah. No later datum in this focus window required abandoning the endpoint reading, but it made that endpoint clearly unauthorized.

## 96:10 — عَبْدًا إِذَا صَلَّىٰٓ

### Activated readings

1. **A servant located inside contested ownership.** `ع ب د:B001` supplies owned status, `ع ب د:B003` worshipful submission, and `ع ب د:B004` the act of reducing someone to slave-like subjection. The servant is the object of another human’s restraint (96:9), yet his act is prayer and return is to the Rabb (96:8). The three branches separate two jurisdictions: devotional belonging to the Rabb and attempted human control of that belonging. The packet gives the prohibition; I infer that it tries to redirect the servant’s obedience. `عبداً` therefore names not social insignificance but the disputed locus of authority.

2. **Prayer as both prescribed act and communicative appeal.** `ص ل و:B003` supplies prescribed worship with bodily limits, while `ص ل و:B002` supplies supplication, praise, blessing, and mercy. Guidance and protective caution follow in 96:11–12, and divine seeing appears in 96:14. The prescribed branch gives the act its embodied discipline; the supplication branch makes it a directed communication under pressure. I infer that restraining the act attempts to interrupt both bodily worship and appeal. `صلى` thus reads as a complete relation, not a gesture isolated from speech and mercy.

3. **Voluntary lowering opposed to coerced subduing.** `ع ب د:B003` assigns submissive obedience to worship, while `ع ب د:B005` supplies physical subduing and smoothing. The servant freely enters prayer; by 96:15 the restrainer faces coercive seizure by the forelock. The packet supplies the two bodily relations, and I infer the contrast in agency: one lowering is chosen devotion, the other imposed humiliation. The fixed `عبداً` consequently distinguishes willing service from the controller’s attempt to make another humanly subdued.

4. **The occasion treated as a snare, then capture reversed (exploratory).** `ص ل و:B004` supplies a set snare, a materially distinct branch from prescribed prayer. The restrainer acts specifically `إذا صلى`, as though prayer supplies the occasion on which to catch or block the servant; 96:15 then redirects capture to the restrainer’s forelock. I infer the snare relation from timing and reversal, not from an explicit trap noun. The dormant branch makes the scene read as a failed ambush whose logic returns upon its initiator.

### Retrospective surprises

96:11–14 confirmed that the servant’s prayer belongs to the guidance-and-seeing relation rather than to the restrainer’s jurisdiction. The surprise was 96:15: coercive forelock seizure reversed the attempted subduing and activated both `ع ب د:B005` and, more tentatively, the snare branch `ص ل و:B004`. That later capture made the distinction between chosen service and imposed humiliation structurally visible.

## 96:11 — أَرَءَيْتَ إِن كَانَ عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ

### Activated readings

1. **A conditional that demands reflective repositioning.** `ر ء ي:B013` supplies the alerting “tell me” formula and `ر ء ي:B002` reflective judgment. Instead of presenting a direct assertion, `أرأيت إن كان` asks the observer to inhabit a possibility concerning the restrained servant. The branch’s role is to turn seeing into reconsideration of the scene’s moral alignment. I infer that the conditional loosens the apparent controller’s framing. The line reads as a demand to judge from another position, not merely to identify who is visible.

2. **Guidance as an occupied position.** `ك و ن:B001` supplies being or occurrence, while `ك و ن:B002` supplies place, position, or rank. The construction does not merely say that guidance exists; it says `كان على الهدى`, using `على` to place the subject upon it. The position branch makes guidance a stance that can be occupied and maintained. I infer stability from the spatial predication. The ayah therefore reads as asking whether the servant stands on a real supporting orientation rather than merely possessing an opinion.

3. **Guidance as a course with a leading edge.** `ه د ي:B002` supplies course, manner, and intended direction, and `ه د ي:B003` supplies the leading front part or guide. Prayer precedes, command to protective caution follows, and 96:13 supplies the opposed movement `تولى`; 96:15–16 then focus the antagonist’s forelock. The guidance branches give the servant’s conduct a directed leading function, while the false “front” is later seized. I infer that embodied worship advances along the course it displays. `على الهدى` thus reads dynamically as being carried by a leading way.

4. **Humanly invited sight corrected by divine sight.** `ر ء ي:B004` supplies mutual visibility. The observer is repeatedly invited to see (96:9, 96:11, 96:13), but 96:14 states that Allah sees. The fixed question places human judgment inside a larger visual relation. I infer that the later clause corrects any uncertainty or partiality in the invited gaze. The ayah’s conditional examination is therefore provisional under a seeing that does not depend on the observer’s answer.

### Retrospective surprises

96:13’s `تولى` supplied the exact opposite vector to `ه د ي:B002`, while 96:15–16 unexpectedly made the “leading front” branch `ه د ي:B003` concrete through the false forelock that is seized. 96:14 also corrected the provisional human `أرأيت` by placing the whole conditional under divine sight. Guidance consequently became both course and trustworthy leading front.

## 96:12 — أَوْ أَمَرَ بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰٓ

### Activated readings

1. **A directive that constructs protection.** `ء م ر:B002` supplies command or directive, and `و ق ي:B002` supplies placing the self in protective moral caution. The directive stands opposite the restrainer’s `ينهى` and is followed by denial, withdrawal, and threatened seizure (96:13–16). The fixed roots assign two roles: `أمر` transmits an obligation, while `التقوى` is the protective state that obligation seeks to produce. The packet supplies the contrast; I infer that compliance builds a guard against the ensuing harmful course. The ayah reads as active protection-making rather than a vague commendation of piety.

2. **Legitimate command confronting rival command.** `ء م ر:B003` supplies authority and the command-holder. One figure forbids prayer (96:9–10); the alternative subject commands protective caution; the antagonist later calls his council (96:17). The authority branch turns `أمر` into a jurisdictional question: which directive deserves social force? I infer from the opposed commands that guidance authorizes the protective directive while self-sufficient transgression produces the rival one. The line therefore contests governance, not only private moral advice.

3. **Taqwa as a barrier against coercive harm.** `و ق ي:B001` supplies a guard or barrier placed between something and injury. Forelock seizure, the lying/wrongdoing characterization, and the council call all lie ahead inside the window (96:15–17). The barrier branch’s role is to make `التقوى` materially defensive: it keeps the commanded person from being absorbed into the denier’s chain of obedience. The packet does not specify the barrier’s mechanism; I infer that guarded conduct blocks moral capture. The noun reads as preserved agency under pressure.

4. **Careful movement along guidance.** `و ق ي:B003` supplies a guarded gait that protects a painful hoof from rough ground. The preceding ayah places the subject `على الهدى`, whose `ه د ي:B002` is a directed course, while 96:13 supplies turning away. The gait branch gives taqwa a locomotor role: proceed attentively so that the path does not injure or divert the walker. I infer the path-and-gait analogy from adjacency. The ayah can thus describe disciplined movement, not static fear.

### Retrospective surprises

96:13’s turning away sharpened the guarded-gait image `و ق ي:B003`: taqwa is careful continuance where the alternative leaves the course. The forelock threat and council call in 96:15–17 then made `و ق ي:B001` a social barrier against capture, and 96:17 made the authority branch `ء م ر:B003` newly relevant by displaying the rival’s own mobilized command structure.

## 96:13 — أَرَءَيْتَ إِن كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ

### Activated readings

1. **The final witness prompt before the gaze is settled.** `ر ء ي:B013` supplies the alerting “tell me” formula, and `ر ء ي:B002` supplies judgment. This third `أرأيت` tests the opposite course from 96:11–12, then 96:14 answers the visual sequence with `الله يرى`. Its role is to make denial and withdrawal evidence for a verdict. I infer a cumulative examination from the repeated form. The line reads as the last question put to a witness before the scene is placed under divine sight.

2. **Active invalidation rather than a merely false utterance.** `ك ذ ب:B002` supplies declaring a person or report false, denying, or disproving; this fits the intensive `كذّب`. Guidance and the command to taqwa are the immediately preceding alternatives, and 96:16 later calls the seized forelock `كاذبة`. The branch’s role is to make the act an attempted cancellation of the guiding claim. I infer continuity from active denial to the later adjective: what was done becomes a property exposed at the bodily front. `كذّب` therefore reads as suppressive rejection whose effect marks the rejecter.

3. **A directional reversal away from the return.** `و ل ي:B006` supplies turning toward and facing, while `و ل ي:B007` supplies turning away and withdrawal. The actual form selects withdrawal, but the paired branches expose the excluded vector. `الرجعى` toward the Rabb (96:8) and being on guidance (96:11) establish one direction; `تولى` reverses from it. I infer a vector relation rather than a mood of indifference. The ayah reads as an enacted reorientation away from the course to which return still points.

4. **Withdrawal from guidance becoming a pivot into rival governance.** `و ل ي:B003` supplies assuming charge and authority. Although `تولى` here activates withdrawal through `و ل ي:B007`, the actor does not simply disappear: 96:17 has him call his council, and 96:18 answers with a counter-call. The authority branch assigns the turn a second role: it pivots from guided obligation toward social self-authorization. I infer that direction from the later mobilization. Turning away thus becomes the first movement of rival rule.

### Retrospective surprises

96:14 settled the repeated witness prompts under a superior gaze. More surprisingly, 96:16 converted the act `كذّب` into the enduring description `ناصية كاذبة`, and 96:17–18 showed that `تولى` was not simple disappearance: it pivoted into council mobilization and thus activated `و ل ي:B003` rival authority. The later sequence made denial bodily attributable and withdrawal politically productive.

## 96:14 — أَلَمْ يَعْلَم بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ

### Activated readings

1. **Knowledge that should itself restrain.** `ع ل م:B001` supplies knowing as a thing becoming clear, and `ر ء ي:B001` supplies sight by eye or insight. The question follows a restrainer who denies and turns away, while `ن ه ي:B003` in the window supplies reason’s proper role of restraining ugly action. The known fact of divine seeing functions as an internal brake the actor has failed to use. The packet gives knowing and seeing; I infer the expected causal arrow from knowledge to restraint. The ayah reads as exposure of culpable non-use of knowledge, not a lack of available disclosure.

2. **Seeing that discriminates and marks the actor.** `ع ل م:B002` supplies a distinguishing mark that identifies and points. Immediately afterward, the forelock is targeted and described as lying and culpably wrong (96:15–16). The mark branch gives `يعلم` an evidentiary role: conduct is distinguishable and attributable under the gaze. I infer that divine seeing supports that identification; the packet does not say that sight physically writes a mark. The line therefore reads as precise recognition that prevents the actor from hiding inside an undifferentiated crowd.

3. **The apparent overseer placed under superior surveillance.** `ر ء ي:B004` supplies mutual visibility and facing. Since 96:9 the antagonist has appeared able to monitor and stop another’s prayer; repeated `أرأيت` turns observers toward him, and `الله يرى` closes the reversal. The branch’s role is to relocate the controlling gaze: the watcher is now the seen object. I infer hierarchy from Allah as grammatical subject. The ayah changes from a generic statement of omnivision into a direct collapse of human surveillance power.

4. **Sight anchored to the worshipped one.** `ء ل ه:B001` supplies the worshipped one, while `ء ل ه:B002` supplies the fixed divine name. The visible conflict concerns a servant at prayer, and the final response will refuse obedience and prostrate (96:19). The divine name’s role here is relational: the one to whom worship is directed is also the one who sees interference with it. I infer protective oversight from that alignment, not an unstated event of intervention. The line reads as sight exercised from within the worship relation, not detached observation.

### Retrospective surprises

96:15–16 supplied the most concrete later surprise: the forelock is singled out and morally described, activating `ع ل م:B002` as discriminatory identification under the gaze. The council and counter-call in 96:17–18 showed that coalition cover does not escape that sight, while 96:19’s prostration confirmed that `الله` is present here as the worshipped one, not merely as an abstract observer.

## 96:15 — كَلَّا لَئِن لَّمْ يَنتَهِ لَنَسْفَعًۢا بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ

### Activated readings

1. **The restrainer made the one who must cease.** `ن ه ي:B001` supplies both restraining another and ceasing oneself. The earlier finite roles reverse across 96:9 and 96:15: the one who `ينهى` a servant is now the subject of `لم ينته`. The root’s role is juridical symmetry, turning his imposed restraint back upon him as a required self-restraint. The packet supplies the morphological recurrence; I infer that the reversal is corrective. The line reads as a transfer of the stopping command from the servant to the controller.

2. **An externally supplied endpoint.** `ن ه ي:B002` supplies the endpoint, edge, or limit reached by a thing. The conditional says that if the actor does not bring his conduct to an end, forelock seizure follows. The endpoint branch makes the threat functionally exact: refusal to stop internally produces an imposed terminus. The packet supplies the conditional sequence; I infer the causal arrow from non-cessation to enforced limit. `ينته` therefore concerns both moral cessation and who controls the action’s endpoint.

3. **Capture at the bodily point of control.** `س ف ع:B001` supplies coercive seizure by the forelock, and `ن ص ي:B001` supplies both the forelock and figurative control through taking it. Their immediate combination removes lexical ambiguity: one root supplies the force and the other the handle and control relation. I infer that bodily capture enacts loss of agency rather than merely accompanying it. The threat thus reads as a concrete reversal of the antagonist’s effort to control another’s worship.

4. **Seizing the social vanguard as well as the head.** `ن ص ي:B003` supplies the chosen elite, noble leaders, or front rank. The next lines characterize the forelock, then let the actor summon his council (96:16–17); a counter-summons follows in 96:18. The elite branch makes the forelock the social “front” of his authority, not only hair. I infer metonymic extension from bodily front to leadership. The threat can therefore announce collapse of a whole prestige structure at its leading point.

### Retrospective surprises

96:16 made the threatened forelock a morally qualified locus of agency rather than a neutral body-part. 96:17–18 then activated `ن ص ي:B003`: the seized “front” is also the leading edge of a council-based power formation answered by another force. Finally, 96:19’s voluntary lowering completed the reversal between imposed capture and chosen submission.

## 96:16 — نَاصِيَةٍۢ كَٰذِبَةٍ خَاطِئَةٍۢ

### Activated readings

1. **Moral agency concentrated in a seized body-part.** `ن ص ي:B001` supplies forelock and control by taking it; `ك ذ ب:B001` supplies falsehood in speech or action; `خ ط ء:B002` supplies culpable wrongdoing. Grammatically, the two agentive adjectives modify the forelock rather than naming the person separately. The forelock therefore serves as the locus where control, falsification, and guilt become graspable. The packet supplies the metonymy; I infer that seizure targets embodied agency. The fragment reads as a diagnosis of the controller at his point of control.

2. **A corrupt leading front.** `ن ص ي:B003` supplies the elite, leaders, forerunners, and select front. The following `فليدع ناديه` mobilizes a council, while 96:18 mobilizes an opposing force. The branch assigns the forelock a collective role: it is the leading face of a social formation whose moral quality is lying and wrongdoing. I infer that the bodily adjective radiates to the leadership it represents. The line thus exposes not only an individual vice but a corrupt vanguard.

3. **Intentional falsehood joined to directional failure.** `ك ذ ب:B001` supplies falsehood against truth; `خ ط ء:B001` supplies missing or passing beyond the right direction, while `خ ط ء:B002` preserves culpability. Guidance as a course appears in 96:11 and protective command in 96:12. The two adjectives therefore do different work: one rejects truth, while the other moves off its proper line, and the culpable branch prevents the deviation from being reduced to accident. I infer the directional relation from the earlier guidance construction. The forelock reads as both epistemically false and deliberately misdirected.

### Retrospective surprises

96:17–18 confirmed the collective reach of `ن ص ي:B003`: the corrupt front calls an assembly and meets a counter-assembly. 96:19 then supplied a bodily correction, contrasting this culpable forelock with a head voluntarily lowered in prostration. The later ayat preserved the distinction between directional error `خ ط ء:B001` and culpable wrongdoing `خ ط ء:B002` rather than collapsing them.

## 96:17 — فَلْيَدْعُ نَادِيَهُۥ

### Activated readings

1. **A call that assembles protective social proximity.** `د ع و:B001` supplies calling in order to draw near, and `ن د و:B001` supplies the council, assembly place, and people gathered there. The imperative makes the first root activate the second: vocal summons is the mechanism by which a dispersed affiliation becomes a nearby body. The packet supplies the call and its object; I infer that proximity is sought for protection or force after the forelock threat. `ناديه` consequently reads as both place and mobilizable social institution.

2. **A claim of affiliation and jurisdiction.** `د ع و:B002` supplies claiming a right, lineage, or affiliation. Calling “his” council immediately after being described as lying and culpably wrong makes the possessive socially consequential. The claim branch’s role is to turn the summons into an assertion that this group belongs to his side and can answer for him. I infer that the call invokes jurisdiction, not merely companionship. The ayah reads as an appeal to coalition identity against the threatened loss of control.

3. **Public voice as a force multiplier.** `ن د و:B002` supplies raised voice, calling, summons, and the carrying reach of sound. The root `د ع و` already names calling, so the object `ناديه` echoes the acoustic mechanism that produces the gathering. The doubled call-field turns private defiance into public mobilization. I infer that reach of voice is what scales the actor’s power. The line therefore exposes how a threatened individual tries to convert speech into collective force.

4. **A summons that calls down its own counterforce.** `د ع و:B004` supplies calling down an unwelcome thing. The jussive `فليدع` is answered by future `سندع` in 96:18, with the latter naming a repelling force. The packet supplies the parallel calls; I infer that exercising the first summons occasions the second. The line can thus read ironically: the attempt to summon help becomes the condition under which an unwelcome answering summons is heard.

### Retrospective surprises

96:18’s exact repetition of `دعو` transformed the first summons into a challenge answered in its own grammar, strongly activating `د ع و:B004` as a call that brings down an unwelcome response. 96:19 then showed the practical failure of the council’s claimed jurisdiction: the addressee is told not to obey him. The later ayat therefore corrected any assumption that gathering itself guarantees authority.

## 96:18 — سَنَدْعُ ٱلزَّبَانِيَةَ

### Activated readings

1. **A superior counter-mobilization.** `د ع و:B001` supplies calling to draw near. The future `سندع` answers the immediately preceding jussive `فليدع`, but the objects differ: the actor calls his council, while the answering speaker calls `الزبانية`. The repeated verb makes the two mobilizations formally comparable; the changed subject and object establish asymmetry. I infer that the second call counters and supersedes the first. The ayah reads as a response in the same social grammar but with greater enforcement capacity.

2. **Repelling force as the agents’ defining function.** `ز ب ن:B001` supplies pushing away, collision, prevention, and defensive resistance. The preceding actor has denied, turned away, faced forelock seizure, and tried to mobilize a council. The branch assigns `الزبانية` a functional role: they push back the transgressive pressure and prevent its coalition from controlling the scene. The packet names the force but not each action; I infer the enforcement direction from the confrontation. The noun becomes a dynamic counter-pressure rather than an unexplained group label.

3. **A second bodily hold completing the forelock image.** `ز ب ن:B008` supplies the neck as a point that can be seized. Forelock seizure in 96:15 has already located control at the front of the head. The neck-hold branch extends that arrest topology: called repellers can immobilize the body whose leading point is already threatened. I infer coordination between the two accepted bodily images; the packet does not narrate a second seizure. The line therefore activates a complete restraint mechanism around head and neck.

4. **Expulsion from the social center (exploratory).** `ز ب ن:B005` supplies distance or withdrawal away from one’s dwellings, while 96:17 invokes `ناديه`, the actor’s assembly place and people. Combined with `ز ب ن:B001` pushing away, the branch can assign the called force a spatial result: removal from the council-space on which the actor relies. I infer this displacement from the opposed place and repulsion images. The ayah may therefore threaten loss of social refuge as well as physical defeat.

### Retrospective surprises

96:19 revealed that the repelling call is not merely a terminal threat: it changes the action conditions so refusal, prostration, and approach become practicable. That later command sharpened `ز ب ن:B001` as defensive counter-pressure and supported the inference that the called force blocks the antagonist’s coalition rather than simply adding another crowd.

## 96:19 — كَلَّا لَا تُطِعْهُ وَٱسْجُدْ وَٱقْتَرِب ۩

### Activated readings

1. **Refusal as a reassignment of obedience.** `ط و ع:B001` supplies pliant obedience and willing yielding; the negative imperative blocks that yielding toward `ـه`. It is immediately coordinated with two positive imperatives, `اسجد` and `اقترب`, so obedience is not abolished but redirected into another embodied relation. The packet supplies the command sequence; I infer reassignment from negative-to-positive coordination. The ayah reads as liberation from a coercive addressee through a different allegiance, not as undirected defiance.

2. **The practical capacity to refuse.** `ط و ع:B003` supplies ability and the conditions that make action possible. The preceding window includes divine seeing, threatened forelock seizure, and a counter-call that answers the antagonist’s council (96:14–18). Those supplied protections alter the action conditions around `لا تطعه`. I infer that the counterforce enables resistance to social coercion. The imperative therefore reads not only as obligation but as an assertion that noncompliance has been made practicable.

3. **Voluntary lowering reversing forced seizure.** `س ج د:B001` supplies humble, obedient lowering, and `س ج د:B004` supplies bowing or lowering the head. The preceding scene threatens coercive seizure of a lying forelock; here the commanded subject lowers the head voluntarily. The same bodily axis carries opposite agency: forced humiliation for the controller, chosen submission for the one refusing him. I infer the reversal from adjacency. `اسجد` thus converts bodily lowering from an instrument of domination into an act of free orientation.

4. **Lowering as the route to nearness.** `ق ر ب:B001` supplies nearness, `ق ر ب:B005` devotional approach, and `ق ر ب:B004` favored access. The coordination `واسجد واقترب` places prostration before approach. The packet supplies sequence but does not explicitly state causation; I infer that prostration serves as the embodied means or expression of approach. `اقترب` consequently reads as gained devotional access rather than ordinary spatial movement.

5. **A contest between social access and devotional access.** `ق ر ب:B004` includes privileged nearness to a ruler or inner circle, while `ق ر ب:B005` includes nearness sought through devotion. The preceding council and counterforce stage competing structures of access and protection. The two variants remain distinct: one reading hears `اقترب` as entry into favor that surpasses council-status; the other hears active devotional approach itself. I infer the contrast from the immediately preceding mobilizations. The final imperative relocates privilege from the antagonist’s social center to nearness achieved through prostration.

### Retrospective surprises

No later ayah exists in this clipped focus window. The retrospective reread therefore added no later-ayah surprise beyond the already visible relations to divine sight, forelock seizure, and the paired summons in 96:14–18.
