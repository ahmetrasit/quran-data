# Surah 15 Full-Context Eleven-Ayah Analytical Walk

## 15:0 — بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

### Activated readings

1. **A named enclosure of care.** Lexically, `س م و` `B005` makes *ism* a raised designation, while `ر ح م` `B001` supplies tenderness and `B003` the womb image. The structural cue is the movement from this naming into signs, a written book, a known register, and a fixed communal term in 15:1–5. The name supplies invocation and the womb branch supplies protective origination. The packet gives naming, mercy, inscription, and timing; I infer that the opening places the ordered disclosure under generative care. The formula thus reads not only as a request for mercy, but as entry into a named, protective order in which what follows is brought forth.

### Retrospective surprises

15:4–5 sharpened the surprise: the later known register and non-negotiable term make the opening mercy compatible with measured order, rather than an alternative to it.

## 15:1 — الٓر ۚ تِلْكَ ءَايَٰتُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ وَقُرْءَانٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ

### Activated readings

1. **A gathered recitation that also binds.** `ك ت ب` `B002` is joined letters and written book, while `B003` is inscription as binding decree; `ق ر ء` `B001` gathers speech into recitation; `ب ي ن` `B005` discloses meaning. The immediate sequence names signs, book, recitation, and disclosure, while 15:4 calls each settlement's term a known *kitab* and 15:5 denies temporal escape. The written and gathered forms therefore serve as both disclosure and binding order. The packet supplies the repeated *kitab* and fixed term; I infer that the later juridical-temporal use activates `B003` here. The ayah reads differently as a clear recited collection whose writtenness carries enforceable order, not as a merely descriptive title.

### Retrospective surprises

15:4 supplied the decisive later activation: its “known book” makes the book of 15:1 resonate with registered limit as well as joined writing.

## 15:2 — رُّبَمَا يَوَدُّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لَوْ كَانُوا۟ مُسْلِمِينَ

### Activated readings

1. **A wish generated by failed temporal transfer.** `و د د` `B002` is wishing for an event; `ك ف ر` `B001` is covering; `س ل م` `B012` is handing over or letting go, alongside `B001` soundness and safety. In 15:3 present enjoyment and hope defer knowledge, while 15:4–5 place communities under a term that cannot be advanced or postponed. The contrast assigns covering the role of present refusal and *islam* the role of a transfer not made in time. The packet gives desire, counterfactual being, concealment, surrender, and an inescapable term; I infer that the later wish arises when control can no longer be handed over voluntarily. “Muslimin” consequently reads as those who had entered safety by relinquishing control before the boundary, not only as a group label.

### Retrospective surprises

15:5 converts the wish into a specifically belated wish: “neither precede nor delay” makes the counterfactual in 15:2 a failed timing relation.

## 15:3 — ذَرْهُمْ يَأْكُلُوا۟ وَيَتَمَتَّعُوا۟ وَيُلْهِهِمُ ٱلْأَمَلُ ۖ فَسَوْفَ يَعْلَمُونَ

### Activated readings

1. **A self-maintaining delay mechanism.** `م ت ع` `B007` is prolonged keeping for benefit, `ل ه و` `B001` diversion from one concern by another occupation, and `ء م ل` `B001` hope held in expectation. Eating, extended enjoyment, diversion, hope, and future knowing form a causal sequence; 15:4–5 then reveal a known, immovable limit, and 15:8 removes respite once the decisive arrival occurs. The lexical branches supply occupations and expectation; I infer the arrow by which repeated benefit feeds diversion and diversion lets hope mask the deadline. The ayah therefore reads as a temporal machine that reproduces postponement, rather than as an unstructured list of pleasures.

2. **Knowledge as delayed unveiling.** `ع ل م` `B001` is knowing as a thing becomes clear. Its future position after the chain contrasts with `معلوم` in 15:4 and the already fixed term in 15:5. The role of knowledge is not to create the limit but to expose a limit already ordered. The packet supplies that asymmetry; I infer that subjective recognition lags behind objective measure. “They will know” thus reads as forced catching-up to prior clarity.

### Retrospective surprises

15:4–5 revealed that the hope in 15:3 does not keep the future open; it only keeps the subjects from seeing a future already measured.

## 15:4 — وَمَآ أَهْلَكْنَا مِن قَرْيَةٍ إِلَّا وَلَهَا كِتَابٌۭ مَّعْلُومٌۭ

### Activated readings

1. **A settlement entered into measured institutional time.** `ق ر ي` `B001` is people gathered in a settlement, `ك ت ب` `B003` a binding inscription or judgment, and `ع ل م` `B002` a distinguishing mark pointing to something. The gathered social unit is paired with its own inscribed, marked limit; 15:5 expresses that limit as neither outrunnable nor deferrable, while 15:9 later describes preservation of the reminder. The settlement is the accountable collective, the book fixes its boundary, and knownness marks it in advance. The packet gives these parts; I infer that destruction follows registered communal time rather than arbitrary interruption. The ayah reads as institutional accounting attached to a social body.

### Retrospective surprises

15:5 makes the book's function explicit by translating registration into a two-sided temporal boundary: no preceding it and no falling behind it.

## 15:5 — مَّا تَسْبِقُ مِنْ أُمَّةٍ أَجَلَهَا وَمَا يَسْتَـْٔخِرُونَ

### Activated readings

1. **A community held inside a non-escapable temporal lane.** `س ب ق` `B004` is outstripping or escaping another, `ء م م` `B004` a community, `ء ج ل` `B001` a fixed term, and `ء خ ر` `B002` postponement. The paired negations reverse the two possible escape directions around the term: racing ahead and deferring behind. The branches assign the community a collective trajectory and the term a boundary. The packet supplies the symmetry; I infer that advance and delay are imagined as attempted exits from one measured lane. The ayah consequently reads spatially as well as temporally: a community cannot leave its appointed position from either side.

### Retrospective surprises

15:8's statement that angelic descent would leave no respite confirms that “not delaying” in 15:5 is an operative closure, not a loose prediction.

## 15:6 — وَقَالُوا۟ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِى نُزِّلَ عَلَيْهِ ٱلذِّكْرُ إِنَّكَ لَمَجْنُونٌۭ

### Activated readings

1. **An accusation that reverses the location of concealment.** `ذ ك ر` `B009` is reminder or admonition, while `ج ن ن` `B006` is madness as a veil over the mind. The speakers acknowledge a descending reminder but recode its receiver as mentally veiled; 15:9 calls that reminder guarded, and 15:11 locates repeated mockery among the receiving groups. The reminder's role is to uncover; the madness charge relocates covering onto its bearer. The packet provides the accusation and the later reception pattern; I infer the ironic reversal that a blocked reception strategy projects concealment onto the messenger. The ayah reads as an attempted neutralization of admonition by renaming disclosure as concealment.

### Retrospective surprises

15:9's preserved reminder and 15:11's recurrent mockery make the charge in 15:6 look less like diagnosis and more like a repeatable reception strategy.

## 15:7 — لَّوْ مَا تَأْتِينَا بِٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ إِن كُنتَ مِنَ ٱلصَّٰدِقِينَ

### Activated readings

1. **A demanded proof that would actually close the test.** `ء ت ي` `B001` is coming or arrival, and `ص د ق` `B004` is confirmation and fulfillment, not only `B001` truth in speech. The demand makes angelic arrival a proposed confirmation; 15:8 immediately says such descent occurs only with *haqq* and would allow no respite. Arrival therefore serves as terminal fulfillment rather than neutral evidence. The packet gives the demand and consequence; I infer that the speakers mistakenly treat a closing event as a reversible experiment. The ayah reads as a hazardous demand to collapse the interval in which truth can still be received.

### Retrospective surprises

15:8 is the correction: the requested arrival would confirm by enacting the due outcome, not by staging a spectacle after which ordinary choice resumes.

## 15:8 — مَا نُنَزِّلُ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةَ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ وَمَا كَانُوٓا۟ إِذًۭا مُّنظَرِينَ

### Activated readings

1. **Descent as due execution.** `ن ز ل` `B006` is a descending calamity, `ح ق ق` `B002` a due obligation or deserved claim, and `ن ظ ر` `B002` watching for a time or granting respite. The restriction “only with haqq” is followed directly by denial of respite; 15:11–13 then portray a settled pattern of mockery and non-assent. The due claim determines the descent's role, while the negated respite marks completion. The packet supplies juxtaposition, but I infer the directional arrow from accumulated refusal to due execution. The ayah therefore reads as a rule of terminal intervention: angelic descent is withheld until it is an enactment of what has become due.

### Retrospective surprises

15:13's inherited pattern of non-assent makes the “due” aspect more visible: the closure answers a sustained trajectory, not a single isolated demand.

## 15:9 — إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا ٱلذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُۥ لَحَٰفِظُونَ

### Activated readings

1. **Preservation through hostile transit and inward reception.** `ذ ك ر` `B009` is admonitory reminder; `ح ف ظ` `B001` is guarding and `B002` retention in mind. The emphatic descent-and-guarding statement sits between denied respite and a sequence in which messengers are mocked, something is inserted into criminal hearts, and assent fails (15:10–13). Guarding protects the reminder's existence; mental retention supplies a second, more exploratory role in its passage through resistant interiors. The packet gives preservation and later heart-entry; I infer that preservation includes surviving hostile transmission without guaranteeing assent. The ayah reads as preservation of an operative reminder, not preservation from ever encountering rejection.

### Retrospective surprises

15:12 activates the inward dimension of `ح ف ظ` `B002`: what is guarded can enter hearts even where 15:13 says it is not trusted.

## 15:10 — وَلَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا مِن قَبْلِكَ فِى شِيَعِ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ

### Activated readings

1. **Mission distributed through recurrent social formations.** `ر س ل` `B005` is succession in batches, `ش ي ع` `B002` a supporting or following party and `B003` spreading or diffusion, and `ق ب ل` `B002` priorness. The prior sending “among parties” is followed by the repeated rule that every messenger is mocked (15:11), then by an inherited first-people pattern in 15:13. Sending supplies recurrence; *shiyaʿ* distributes it through factional formations. The packet gives plurality and repetition; I infer that the history is a replicated social process rather than a row of unrelated biographies. The ayah reads as a network of missions and factional receptions.

### Retrospective surprises

15:11's “no messenger except” turns `ر س ل` `B005` from possible succession into a visibly repeated batch-pattern of sending and response.

## 15:11 — وَمَا يَأْتِيهِم مِّن رَّسُولٍ إِلَّا كَانُوا۟ بِهِۦ يَسْتَهْزِءُونَ

### Activated readings

1. **Mockery as a habitual reception protocol.** `ه ز ء` `B001` is derision, `ر س ل` `B002` messenger and message, and `ك و ن` `B001` supports occurrence or temporal predication. The universal “no messenger except” follows prior missions among factions and precedes heart-entry and inherited non-assent in 15:12–13. Mockery therefore serves as the recurring operation applied when a message arrives. The packet gives quantification and sequence; I infer a stable social protocol rather than spontaneous humor. The ayah reads as a durable mechanism for preventing a messenger's arrival from becoming assent.

### Retrospective surprises

15:12–13 relocate the effect of mockery inward: the repeated public act becomes a pattern carried through hearts and inherited practice.

## 15:12 — كَذَٰلِكَ نَسْلُكُهُۥ فِى قُلُوبِ ٱلْمُجْرِمِينَ

### Activated readings

1. **The prior reception pattern threaded into the inward faculty.** `س ل ك` `B002` is letting something enter into something, `ق ل ب` `B001` the inward faculty and `B004` turning or reversal, and `ج ر م` `B003` earning or causing oneself to incur. The demonstrative reaches back to repeated mockery in 15:11, and 15:13 supplies the result, non-assent. Mockery-pattern is the entering element, the heart is its channel, and earned offense names the receptive group. The packet leaves the pronoun's exact referent unstated; I abductively take the nearest reception pattern as one viable input. The ayah then reads as social derision becoming an internalized perceptual route that turns what arrives.

2. **The reminder enters without becoming trusted.** A coexisting reading takes the pronoun from the guarded *dhikr* of 15:9. The same `س ل ك` `B002` makes entry possible, while `ق ل ب` `B004` and 15:13's explicit non-assent explain why entry is not acceptance. Here the reminder is the entering element and the turned heart blocks confirmation. The packet supplies reminder, entry, and non-belief; I infer only the causal obstruction between entry and assent. This reading preserves the distinction between exposure and reception.

### Retrospective surprises

15:13 is the later discriminator but not a disambiguator: “they do not trust it” supports both an internalized rejection-pattern and a reminder that enters without securing assent.

## 15:13 — لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِهِۦ ۖ وَقَدْ خَلَتْ سُنَّةُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ

### Activated readings

1. **Non-assent as an inherited running way.** `ء م ن` `B002` is trusting assent and confirmation, `س ن ن` `B001` a running way or followed practice, and `خ ل و` `B003` passing away or elapsing. The ayah joins present non-assent to a prior people's already-passed pattern; 15:14–15 then show that even an opened sky and ascent are recoded rather than trusted. The *sunnah* supplies a traveled behavioral channel, while non-assent is its renewed outcome. The packet gives the historical link and the coming sensory counterexample; I infer that practice governs interpretation before evidence arrives. The ayah reads as inherited epistemic procedure, not simple absence of information.

### Retrospective surprises

15:14–15 provide the test case: extraordinary seeing does not break the running way, because the viewers rename opened perception as blockage or enchantment.

## 15:14 — وَلَوْ فَتَحْنَا عَلَيْهِم بَابًۭا مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ فَظَلُّوا۟ فِيهِ يَعْرُجُونَ

### Activated readings

1. **A physical opening that fails to become an inward opening.** `ف ت ح` `B001` is opening a closed aperture and `B008` an inner opening by insight; `ب و ب` `B004` is a frontier; `ع ر ج` `B004` is ascent. The packet supplies a literal sky-door and continued ascent, then 15:15 supplies continued denial; 15:16–18 show the sky as ornamented, guarded architecture. The door admits bodies and sight across a frontier, but the dormant inner-opening branch remains unrealized. I infer from the failed response that physical access does not cause insight. The ayah reads as a controlled contrast between traversable space and unopened understanding.

### Retrospective surprises

15:15 activates the negative counterpart of `ف ت ح` `B008`: ascent continues, yet the speakers describe their sight as closed down.

## 15:15 — لَقَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّمَا سُكِّرَتْ أَبْصَٰرُنَا بَلْ نَحْنُ قَوْمٌۭ مَّسْحُورُونَ

### Activated readings

1. **Expanded perception defensively recoded as constriction.** `س ك ر` `B005` is a constricting hold that chokes or pins down, `ب ص ر` `B002` is inner sight, and `س ح ر` `B002` magic as diversion or deception. The preceding ayah supplies opened space and ascent; 15:16 supplies celestial display for viewers. The speakers assign constriction to their sight and deception to their whole group, thereby reversing the evidence's opening function. The packet gives the reversal; I infer a defensive causal strategy in which dissonant perception is neutralized by diagnosing the perceiver. The ayah reads as an epistemic containment maneuver, not merely an exclamation of amazement.

### Retrospective surprises

15:16's deliberate adornment “for viewers” sharpens the reversal: the faculty offered a readable display is precisely the faculty they declare pinned down.

## 15:16 — وَلَقَدْ جَعَلْنَا فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ بُرُوجًۭا وَزَيَّنَّٰهَا لِلنَّٰظِرِينَ

### Activated readings

1. **Beauty and fortification in one visible architecture.** `ب ر ج` `B002` is heavenly mansions or constellations and `B001` fortified towers; `ز ي ن` `B002` makes beauty show; `ن ظ ر` `B004` is what appears pleasing to a viewer. The sky-door and ascent of 15:14 precede this display, while 15:17–18 immediately add guarding, exclusion, and pursuit. Constellations make the architecture visible, adornment attracts sight, and the tower branch gives the same structures a defensive role. The packet supplies beauty followed by guarding; I infer that display and control are co-functions rather than competing descriptions. The sky reads as an inspectable but regulated domain.

### Retrospective surprises

15:17–18 activate `ب ر ج` `B001`: the mansions are not only ornaments, because the next operations are guarding a perimeter and intercepting intrusion.

## 15:17 — وَحَفِظْنَٰهَا مِن كُلِّ شَيْطَٰنٍۢ رَّجِيمٍ

### Activated readings

1. **Guarding as enforced informational distance.** `ح ف ظ` `B001` is preservation and guarding, `ش ط ن` `B001` distance or remoteness and `B004` a rebellious being, while `ر ج م` `B003` is expulsion and banishment. The fortified, adorned sky of 15:16 is followed by the attempted secret listening and pursuing flame of 15:18; 15:21 later places stores under controlled release. Guarding maintains distance, and banishment restores it after attempted breach. The packet gives perimeter, intrusion, and pursuit; I infer that the protected object includes access to an ordered information domain. The ayah reads as active boundary maintenance rather than passive safekeeping.

### Retrospective surprises

15:18 makes the boundary operational: secret listening triggers a follower, showing `ر ج م` `B003` as restored exclusion after intrusion.

## 15:18 — إِلَّا مَنِ ٱسْتَرَقَ ٱلسَّمْعَ فَأَتْبَعَهُۥ شِهَابٌۭ مُّبِينٌۭ

### Activated readings

1. **Unauthorized extraction that generates its own visible audit trail.** `س ر ق` `B002` is secret listening, `ت ب ع` `B002` catching up, `ش ه ب` `B002` a bright flame, and `ب ي ن` `B004` appearance and becoming clear. The exception breaches 15:17's guarded boundary, and the response converts a hidden act into a pursuit by something manifest; 15:21–22 later contrast guarded stores with authorized measured release. Secret listening supplies concealment, following supplies interception, and the bright-clear flame supplies exposure. The packet gives this sequence; I infer the enforcement arrow from breach to disclosure. The ayah reads as a security mechanism in which covert access produces a public trace.

### Retrospective surprises

15:21–22 sharpen the access distinction: stored contents do descend, but by known measure and authorized sending, unlike the extraction attempted here.

## 15:19 — وَٱلْأَرْضَ مَدَدْنَٰهَا وَأَلْقَيْنَا فِيهَا رَوَٰسِىَ وَأَنۢبَتْنَا فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ شَىْءٍۢ مَّوْزُونٍۢ

### Activated readings

1. **An engineered tension of extension, anchoring, and proportioned emergence.** `م د د` `B001` is lengthwise extension, `ر س و` `B001` settling firm, `ن ب ت` `B001` plant growth emerging from earth, and `و ز ن` `B008` a measured, proportioned thing. The verbs move from spreading to casting anchors to making growth emerge; 15:20–22 then connect livelihoods, stores, measured descent, wind, and water. Extension supplies usable space, fixed masses stabilize it, and measure governs emergence. The packet provides the ordered material operations; I infer that stabilization enables sustained proportioned growth. The ayah reads as a functional earth-system, not a catalog of landscape features.

### Retrospective surprises

15:21's “only by a known measure” confirms that *mawzun* is a governing release principle, while 15:22 supplies the water-and-wind process by which measured growth becomes possible.

## 15:20 — وَجَعَلْنَا لَكُمْ فِيهَا مَعَٰيِشَ وَمَن لَّسْتُمْ لَهُۥ بِرَٰزِقِينَ

### Activated readings

1. **Livelihood without total provisioning control.** `ع ي ش` `B002` is means of living, while `ر ز ق` `B001` is provision as an allotted gift. The ayah first assigns human livelihoods within the earth and then places other dependents outside human provisioning; 15:21 locates stores “with Us,” 15:22 gives humans water while denying that they store it, and 15:23 names God as final inheritor. Livelihood supplies human participation, but allotment remains outside human ownership. The packet gives this asymmetry; I infer that co-residents are sustained through the same larger system rather than through human control. The ayah reads as a decentering of economic agency: humans live from the system but do not administer all life within it.

### Retrospective surprises

15:22 repeats the structure almost experimentally: humans receive drink yet are explicitly not its keepers, clarifying the limit already placed on *raziqin* here.

## 15:21 — وَإِن مِّن شَىْءٍ إِلَّا عِندَنَا خَزَآئِنُهُۥ وَمَا نُنَزِّلُهُۥٓ إِلَّا بِقَدَرٍۢ مَّعْلُومٍۢ

### Activated readings

1. **A controlled-release economy.** `خ ز ن` `B001` is storing and safeguarding, `ن ز ل` `B004` placing in proper order as well as `B002` sending down, `ق د ر` `B001` measure reaching a thing's extent, and `ع ل م` `B001` knowledge as clarity. Stores, descent, measure, and knownness are arranged as inventory followed by regulated release; 15:22 instantiates the system with wind and water, and 15:23 extends its effects to life and death. The store preserves potential, measure limits release, and ordered descent delivers it. The packet supplies the sequence; I infer the operational relation between these stages. The ayah thus reads as a supply system whose abundance is real precisely because release is proportioned, not indiscriminate.

### Retrospective surprises

15:22 turns the abstract system into a causal chain, and 15:23 shows that its scope exceeds commodities: measured provision feeds the cycle of life, death, and succession.

## 15:22 — وَأَرْسَلْنَا ٱلرِّيَٰحَ لَوَٰقِحَ فَأَنزَلْنَا مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءًۭ فَأَسْقَيْنَٰكُمُوهُ وَمَآ أَنتُمْ لَهُۥ بِخَٰزِنِينَ

### Activated readings

1. **Authorized release through a generative chain.** `ر س ل` `B001` is sending forth or releasing, `ر و ح` `B003` wind and moving air, `ل ق ح` `B001` fertilization, `ن ز ل` `B002` sending down, `س ق ي` `B001` giving drink, and `خ ز ن` `B001` safeguarding stores. The grammatical sequence explicitly orders release, fertilizing motion, water descent, and delivered drink, before denying human storage. Each branch performs one causal role. The packet supplies the stages; the inferred arrows are that wind enables fertilization and descending water enables drinking. The ayah reads as a managed ecological transfer whose recipients are beneficiaries rather than owners of the reservoir.

### Retrospective surprises

15:23 broadens the chain's consequence: the water process is not an isolated weather image but part of the authority that gives life, gives death, and remains after recipients pass.

## 15:23 — وَإِنَّا لَنَحْنُ نُحْىِۦ وَنُمِيتُ وَنَحْنُ ٱلْوَٰرِثُونَ

### Activated readings

1. **Life and death as transfers within enduring custody.** `ح ي ي` `B002` is earth brought alive by rain and growth, `م و ت` `B001` loss of vitality, and `و ر ث` `B001` possession passing from former holders to an heir. The rain-and-growth chain of 15:19–22 activates the ecological life branch; 15:24 then names those forward and behind, and 15:25 regathers them. Giving life begins a tenure, death ends it, and inheritance names what remains beyond holders. The packet gives these successive operations; I infer a custody cycle connecting them. The ayah reads not only as three divine powers but as one system in which temporary possessors pass while ultimate custody persists.

### Retrospective surprises

15:24–25 make inheritance social and sequential: earlier and later occupants are both known and then gathered, so no generation becomes the final holder.

## 15:24 — وَلَقَدْ عَلِمْنَا ٱلْمُسْتَقْدِمِينَ مِنكُمْ وَلَقَدْ عَلِمْنَا ٱلْمُسْتَـْٔخِرِينَ

### Activated readings

1. **A fully known procession, front and rear.** `ق د م` `B004` is forward advance and precedence, while `ء خ ر` `B003` is the rear or posterior part and `B002` deferral. Repeated `ع ل م` `B001` brackets both classes; 15:23 has just framed generations as temporary holders, and 15:25 says they will be driven together. The advanced and rear positions form one procession rather than independent moral labels. The packet gives symmetrical knowing and later gathering; I infer that historical succession is spatialized as a queue whose entire depth is visible. The ayah reads as comprehensive knowledge of position within succession, including those who appear ahead and those still held behind.

### Retrospective surprises

15:25 collapses the apparent distance between front and rear by gathering both, revealing that the doubled knowledge prepares for a single convergence.

## 15:25 — وَإِنَّ رَبَّكَ هُوَ يَحْشُرُهُمْ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ حَكِيمٌ عَلِيمٌۭ

### Activated readings

1. **Dispersed succession driven back into one judged whole.** `ح ش ر` `B001` is gathering and driving a group to a destination, `ح ك م` `B003` rightly guided wisdom, and `ع ل م` `B001` clear knowledge. The prior ayah distinguishes forward and rear positions; 15:26–30 then moves from differentiated materials and beings to a collective prostration. Gathering removes dispersal, wisdom orders the destination, and knowledge encompasses the members. The packet supplies known positions and convergence; I infer that regathering is a restoration of scattered succession to an ordered whole. The ayah therefore reads as purposeful collection, not mere crowding.

### Retrospective surprises
