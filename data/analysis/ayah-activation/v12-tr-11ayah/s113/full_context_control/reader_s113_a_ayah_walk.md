# Surah 113 Full-Context Eleven-Ayah Ayah Walk

For every fixed ayah, the boundary-clipped eleven-ayah focus window is the full packet, 113:0-113:5.

## 113:0 - بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

### Activated readings

1. **The designation becomes a spoken protective act.** Lexically, fixed **س م و B005** gives *اسم* the role of a raised designation, while fixed **ء ل ه B002** supplies the divine name as an invocation formula; 113:1 adds **ق و ل B001** (uttered speech) and **ع و ذ B002** (protective invocation). Structurally, the naming formula immediately precedes the command *قُلْ* and the first-person *أَعُوذُ*. The packet supplies designation, utterance, and invocation; I infer the arrow by which voicing the designation places the ensuing refuge under the named authority. The basmalah thus reads not as a label before the surah but as the first enactment of its protective speech.

2. **Mercy frames refuge as containing, generative care.** Fixed **ر ح م B001** supplies tenderness and beneficence, and dormant fixed **ر ح م B003** supplies the womb as a vessel of growth. These are activated by 113:1 **ع و ذ B004** (clinging close beside a shelter), **ر ب ب B002** (stage-by-stage nurture), and **ف ل ق B003** (bringing creation forth through splitting). The sequence moves from the doubled mercy-root to shelter, nurture, and emergence. The packet supplies those spatial and generative images; I infer that the one brought forth remains vulnerable and is protected by the same containing care. Without making the divine names a literal womb, the fixed ayah now presents mercy as shelter that both generates and preserves life, not only as pardon.

3. **The doubled mercy-root establishes a bond resistant to hostile subtraction.** Fixed **ر ح م B002** supplies kinship bond and fixed **ر ح م B001** beneficence; later 113:4 **ع ق د B006** supplies firm attachment, whereas 113:5 **ح س د B001** supplies the wish or effort to remove another's blessing. Repetition of the mercy-root before the later chain of threats gives mercy both affective and relational roles. The packet supplies bond, firmness, beneficence, and removal; I infer the opposition in which hostile action tries to undo a sustained good. The two mercy names therefore read as a stable beneficent relation under which refuge is sought, not as two interchangeable ornaments.

### Retrospective surprises

113:4 unexpectedly strengthened the shelter reading: **ع ق د B017** contains an idiom of turning the neck in refuge, so the bodily attachment latent in **ر ح م B003** no longer stands alone. Then 113:5 **ح س د B001**, removal of another's blessing, sharpened the doubled mercy-root retrospectively into beneficence that preserves a vulnerable good against subtraction. The later ayat therefore made the opening formula more structurally protective than it first appeared.

## 113:1 - قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ

### Activated readings

1. **Utterance is itself the counter-action.** Fixed **ق و ل B001** supplies articulated speech and fixed **ع و ذ B002** supplies a protective invocation. Later 113:4 places **ن ف ث B001**, a mouth-blow with slight spittle, inside spell-binding **ع ق د B013**. The structural cue is a contrast between two mouth-mediated acts: commanded speech opens the surah's response, while repeated blowing appears among the harms. The packet supplies both vocal emission and harmful mouth emission; I infer that one voiced act counters or refuses the force claimed by the other. *قُلْ* consequently reads as the performance of refuge, not merely an instruction to report it.

2. **Refuge is movement through an opening rather than retreat into closure.** Fixed **ع و ذ B001** supplies turning for protection, **ع و ذ B004** clinging beside a shelter, **ف ل ق B001** cleaving/opening, and **ف ل ق B002** dawn's disclosure. In 113:3, **غ س ق B001** is darkness settling and **و ق ب B002** is entry into concealment. The packet gives a reversal: opening/disclosure in the fixed ayah, then darkness entering and hiding. I infer that concealment enables danger and that the master of opening can provide an exit or expose what entered. *رَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ* thus names authority over threatened thresholds and reversals, making refuge a passage out of enclosure and obscurity.

3. **Protection continues the work of creation.** Fixed **ر ب ب B002** assigns *رب* the role of nurturing, repairing, and completing stage by stage; fixed **ف ل ق B003** assigns *الفلق* the emergence of created things through splitting. The next ayah adds **خ ل ق B002**, originating and bringing into being, while 113:5 **ح س د B001** seeks the removal of a blessing. The packet supplies emergence, creation, nurture, and attempted removal; I infer that what is brought forth requires continued preservation against forces that would diminish it. Refuge therefore reads as sustaining and completing created good, not only blocking an external blow.

4. **Exploratory: beneficent firmness is contrasted with hostile binding.** Dormant fixed **ر ب ب B016** contains both benefaction and a firm-knot image. It is activated by 113:4 **ع ق د B001** (physical knots) and **ع ق د B013** (spell-binding knots), and by 113:5 **ح س د B001** (attacking another's blessing). The packet supplies this unusual lexical overlap; I infer a contrast between firmness that secures a benefit under *رب* and binding that tries to compromise that benefit. The weak link is that B016 belongs to derived *ربى/رابة* usages rather than the direct noun *رب*. Retained as exploratory, it gives *رب* a latent counter-image to the hostile knots later in the surah.

### Retrospective surprises

The sharpest later surprise came from 113:4: the very root for hostile *العقد* also has **ع ق د B017**, turning toward someone for refuge. That echo corrected a simple refuge-versus-threat opposition into a contrast between two attachments, one to the opener and one to a binding that may claim protective force. 113:5 then activated the benefaction side of exploratory **ر ب ب B016**, making the later knot/benefit overlap visible without removing its derivational weakness.

## 113:2 - مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ

### Activated readings

1. **The general harm is unfolded as harmful operation, not as a claim that creation is itself evil.** Fixed **ش ر ر B001** supplies evil or badness and fixed **خ ل ق B002** originating creation. The three following ayat repeat *وَمِن شَرِّ* and then specify darkness when it enters, blowers acting in knots, and an envier when envy is enacted. The packet supplies the broad *ما*, the repeated construction, and those condition/action structures; I infer that the later items unpack ways created agents, conditions, or capacities become harmful. The fixed ayah consequently reads as refuge from harm arising within the created field while leaving creation and harmful actualization distinct.

2. **Created form includes an inward disposition whose danger appears in enactment.** Dormant fixed **خ ل ق B004** supplies inner character or disposition, while 113:5 **ح س د B001** supplies a removal-seeking disposition and repeats it as agent *حَاسِد* plus action *حَسَدَ*. The structural cue is the movement from the broad created object in 113:2 to a person/action pair under *إذا* in 113:5. The packet supplies character, actor, and act; I infer that a disposition housed in a created person is not identical to the harm it produces when directed and enacted. *مَا خَلَقَ* thus acquires an interior moral dimension in addition to visible created forms.

3. **Harm exploits the affordances of measured creation.** Fixed **خ ل ق B001** supplies measuring and proportioning, and **خ ل ق B003** supplies completed visible form. Around it, 113:1 **ف ل ق B001** opens a division, 113:3 **و ق ب B001** supplies a receiving recess and **و ق ب B002** entry, and 113:4 **ع ق د B001** fastens ends. These packet relations describe openings, cavities, and joins within formed things; I supply the causal arrow that harmful action can use those designed thresholds to penetrate or bind. The reading shifts from an undifferentiated inventory of dangerous objects to a mechanism in which ordinary structures and transitions can be turned toward harm.

4. **Exploratory: created harm is joined by fabricated harmful constructs.** Dormant fixed **خ ل ق B007** supplies fabricated false speech. It is activated by 113:1 **ق و ل B001** (utterance), 113:4 **ن ف ث B001** (mouth emission), **ع ق د B007** (tangled speech), and **ع ق د B013** (spell-binding). The packet supplies speech, emission, and bound spells; I infer that contrived words can be given a material or ritual carrier. The direct perfect *خَلَقَ* and its proximity to *رب الفلق* favor ordinary creation, so this remains exploratory; nevertheless, the fixed phrase can now cast a secondary shadow toward harms people fabricate rather than merely encounter.

### Retrospective surprises

113:5 supplied the decisive correction to an object-only reading of *مَا خَلَقَ*: its noun-plus-verb repetition made dormant fixed **خ ل ق B004** (inner disposition) visible and separated a created bearer from harmful enactment. 113:4 also unexpectedly activated **خ ل ق B007** (fabricated speech) through mouth emission and spell-binding, although the direct form *خَلَقَ* keeps that second relation exploratory.

## 113:3 - وَمِن شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ

### Activated readings

1. **The dangerous moment is ingress, not darkness treated as a static substance.** Fixed **غ س ق B001** supplies night darkness settling in, and fixed **و ق ب B002** supplies entry into concealment; the conditional *إِذَا* joins the actor-state to the event. Earlier 113:1 **ف ل ق B002** supplies dawn splitting open and disclosure after obscurity. The packet supplies opposed transitions, disclosure and concealed entry; I infer that concealment enables otherwise detectable harms. The fixed ayah now concentrates on the threshold at which obscurity penetrates a place or situation, rather than condemning every state of darkness indiscriminately.

2. **A flow enters a receptive hollow and can be retained there.** Fixed **غ س ق B004** supplies flowing or pouring, including night pouring over terrain, while fixed **و ق ب B001** supplies a recess that receives something. In 113:4, **ن ف ث B001** supplies emitted moisture or venom, **ن ف ث B002** emitted blood, **ع ق د B001** fastening, and **ع ق د B003** liquid becoming firm. The packet supplies flow, cavity, emission, and fixation; I infer a transport chain in which a subtle medium becomes harmful when it enters a receiving site and is held there. *غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ* therefore gains a material reading of seepage or infiltration alongside nocturnal entry.

3. **Obscured sight becomes a condition for harmful perception.** Fixed **غ س ق B003** supplies an eye losing brightness, and fixed **و ق ب B001** includes the eye socket among its recesses. This reverses 113:1 **ف ل ق B002**, clarification after obscurity, and is sharpened by 113:5 **ح س د B001**, whose scope begins with seeing another's blessing and wishing its removal. The packet supplies darkened sight and hostile seeing; I infer that perceptual failure can be ethical as well as optical, allowing a seen good to be recoded as a target. The fixed ayah can thus be read as refuge from a mode of vision darkened in what it makes of what it sees.

### Retrospective surprises

113:4 changed the fixed pair most strongly: **ن ف ث B001** and **ن ف ث B002**, together with **ع ق د B001** and **ع ق د B003**, turned flowing **غ س ق B004** plus receptive **و ق ب B001** into a delivery, entry, and retention sequence. 113:5 added a different surprise: because **ح س د B001** begins with seeing another's blessing, fixed **غ س ق B003** (the eye darkening) acquired an ethical-perceptual relation, not just an optical one.

## 113:4 - وَمِن شَرِّ ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ فِى ٱلْعُقَدِ

### Activated readings

1. **Emission and knot form a delivery-and-retention mechanism.** Fixed **ن ف ث B001** supplies a repeated light mouth-blow with slight spittle, including a charmer's or sorceress's blowing; fixed **ع ق د B001** supplies physical fastening and fixed **ع ق د B013** spell-binding knots. The intensive/plural *ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ* and the locative *فِى* make repeated emission enter specific bound sites. Earlier **و ق ب B001** supplies a receiving recess and **و ق ب B002** the model of entry. The packet supplies emission, entry, and binding; I infer that the knot gives a transient breath or droplet persistence by receiving and retaining it. The ayah thus reads as a coupled technology of delivery and storage, not a loose juxtaposition of blowing and knots.

2. **Vented inward pressure can become speech that fixes or tangles another's interior world.** Fixed **ن ف ث B003** supplies a chest-burdened person giving vent; fixed **ع ق د B006** supplies fixed conviction or attachment and fixed **ع ق د B007** tangled, obstructed speech. Earlier 113:1 **ق و ل B001** makes utterance salient, while 113:5 **ح س د B001** supplies a hostile inward desire that may seek removal of another's good. The packet supplies inward pressure, expression, speech, conviction, and hostile desire; I infer the social arrow by which vented affect becomes repeated language that binds belief, attachment, or understanding. The fixed ayah thereby admits a psychosocial mechanism of harmful speech in addition to a physical ritual mechanism.

3. **A subtle discharge can harden into a durable obstruction.** Fixed **ن ف ث B001** supplies expelled moisture or venom and fixed **ن ف ث B002** wound-blood, while fixed **ع ق د B003** supplies liquid thickening or hardening. The preceding ayah's **غ س ق B004** provides flow and **و ق ب B001** a receiving hollow. The packet gives successive material states; I infer that entry plus time or repetition converts a mobile discharge into something fixed. On this reading, the danger lies in a small emission becoming materially or analogically consolidated in the knot.

4. **Exploratory: hostile binding can masquerade as refuge.** Dormant fixed **ع ق د B017** supplies the idiom of bending the neck toward someone for refuge. It is directly activated by 113:1 **ع و ذ B001** (turning for protection) and **ع و ذ B004** (clinging close), while the fixed ayah's overt knot senses are **ع ق د B001** and **ع ق د B013**. The packet supplies both refuge and hostile binding under the same root-field; I infer a contrast between attachment to the true source of opening and attachment to a bound charm that claims protective force. The idiomatic/derived status of B017 makes this exploratory, but it changes *العقد* into possible counterfeit shelters rather than merely hostile objects.

### Retrospective surprises

113:5 made fixed **ن ف ث B003**, giving vent to what burdens the chest, newly consequential: **ح س د B001** provides a specific hostile pressure that can be expressed and then fixed through **ع ق د B006** as attachment or conviction and **ع ق د B007** as tangled speech. This later activation adds a psychosocial mechanism but does not replace the fixed ayah's explicit material and spell-binding branches.

## 113:5 - وَمِن شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ

### Activated readings

1. **The conditional marks the crossing from disposition to harmful enactment.** Fixed **ح س د B001** includes seeing another's blessing, wishing its removal, and possibly acting to remove it. The same root appears first as agent *حَاسِد* and then as finite act *حَسَدَ* under *إِذَا*, matching 113:3's actor/state plus conditional event. The packet supplies the disposition, actor, action, and repeated conditional pattern; I infer that the threat becomes acute when inward orientation turns operative. The ayah therefore reads dynamically: refuge is sought from removal-seeking envy at its moment of activation, not from a static label attached to a person.

2. **The non-dispossessive branch corrects an overbroad account of the threat.** Fixed **ح س د B002** supplies emulative desire for a like blessing without wishing the owner to lose it, whereas fixed **ح س د B001** supplies dispossession and removal. Their shared root is placed under **ش ر ر B001** and repeated as action. The packet supplies both accepted branches and the explicit harmful frame; I infer that direction toward another's loss, not comparison alone, is what makes the mechanism cohere. B002 therefore functions as a lexical boundary: the fixed ayah does not require every wish for comparable good to operate by the evil mechanism described in B001.

3. **Envy is an anti-nurture project aimed at undoing bestowed good.** Fixed **ح س د B001** gives the wished or attempted removal of a blessing. The surah begins with **ر ح م B001** beneficence, then names **ر ب ب B002** nurture and completion, **ف ل ق B003** the bringing forth of created things, and **خ ل ق B002** originating creation. The packet supplies a produced, nurtured benefit and a will to subtract it; I infer that another's flourishing is the object connecting those poles. The final ayah thus reads as a social counter-project to the surah's opening movement of mercy, creation, and preservation, rather than as a private feeling with no causal reach.

4. **Earlier hidden and binding harms can become modalities of enacted envy.** Fixed **ح س د B001** explicitly allows active pursuit of another's loss. Earlier 113:3 supplies concealed entry through **و ق ب B002**, and 113:4 supplies subtle emission through **ن ف ث B001**, physical binding through **ع ق د B001**, and spell-binding through **ع ق د B013**. The packet does not identify one agent across these ayat; I supply the abductive possibility that concealment, insinuation, or binding are means by which removal-seeking envy can act. On this retained reading, *إِذَا حَسَدَ* retrospectively gathers the preceding threats into possible operations of a hostile social intention without reducing them to that intention.

5. **Upward comparison can fork into emulation or subtraction.** Fixed **ح س د B002** supplies non-dispossessive emulation and fixed **ح س د B001** dispossessive envy. The opening *اسم* carries dormant **س م و B007**, rivaling by vying upward, and **س م و B005**, raised designation; the final ayah closes the packet with a person reacting to another's visible blessing. The packet supplies upward rivalry and the two directions of envy; I infer comparison as the shared pivot that can seek either a like good or the removal of the other's good. The fixed repetition *حَاسِدٍ ... حَسَدَ* reads as the point where comparison takes its harmful, subtractive branch while the emulative branch remains visibly distinct.

### Retrospective surprises

No later ayah exists in the boundary-clipped focus window, so no later-ayah surprise emerged.
